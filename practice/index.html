recognise the type

in the exam you are never told which method to use. you look at the equation and decide. here you get instant feedback on the label and on why the near-misses are not.

the seven answers

one of these is right. learn the tell, not the label.

methodthe one-line tell
variable separable every x sits with dx, every y sits with dy
homogeneous (y = vx) quotient of same-degree pieces — only y/x survives
linear (integrating factor) y and y′ appear to power 1 and never multiplied
Bernoulli (z = y^(1−n)) linear, except for one extra yⁿ on the right
reducible by substitution a blob like ax + by + c, or f(y) riding on y′
exact / by inspection M dx + N dy = 0 with ∂M/∂y = ∂N/∂x
none of these the tell is missing — look closer before you commit

    the simplest move in the subject: get every x with dx, every y with dy, then integrate. plus the ax + by + c trick.

    homogeneous equations (y = vx)

    why does one substitution work? because the equation only sees the ratio y/x — and v = y/x is exactly that ratio.

    linear first-order equations

    the integrating factor is not a formula to memorise — it is the answer to a one-line question, derived in front of you.

    Bernoulli, generalised linear, and Clairaut: three disguises that collapse into forms you already know.

    exact equations and inspection

    recognise a perfect differential on sight: d(xy), d(y/x), d(atan(y/x)) — and integrate without doing any work.

    orthogonal trajectories

    find the family that crosses every curve of your family at a right angle — one substitution turns slopes upside down.
