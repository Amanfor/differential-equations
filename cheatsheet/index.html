order & degree rules

  • order: order of the highest derivative; always defined; a positive integer
  • degree: power of the highest derivative AFTER clearing radicals and fractions so the equation is a polynomial in its derivatives
  • degree undefined: the derivative is trapped inside sin,cos,ln,exp and cannot be isolated algebraically
  • essential constants: constants that combine (c1​+c2​, c1​c2​, ec1​+c2​) count as ONE — order = essential constants

orthogonal trajectories

  • cartesian: form the parameter-free DE of the family, then replace y′→−1/y′
  • polar: form the DE in (r,θ), then replace dr/dθ→−r2dθ/dr
  • polar angle: tanϕ=rdrdθ​; perpendicular means ϕ2​=ϕ1​+π/2⟹tanϕ2​=−cotϕ1​

the seven mistakes almost everyone makes

  • declaring the degree undefined whenever trig or exp appear — the degree is undefined ONLY if the derivative itself is inside the transcendental function
  • counting every ci​ as independent — constants that combine algebraically merge — order = number of essential constants
  • forgetting to divide by the coefficient of y′ before computing the I.F. — normalise to y′+P(x)y=Q(x) first, then I.F. =e∫Pdx
  • forcing an equation to be linear in y when it is linear in x — invert: dx/dy−y1​x=yf(y)​ is immediately linear in x
  • dropping absolute values in ln∣x∣ too early — keep them through integration; drop only after the initial condition fixes the sign
  • replacing y′ with 1/y′ for orthogonal trajectories — perpendicular slopes multiply to −1, so the substitution is y′→−1/y′
  • assuming every Clairaut solution comes from p=C — the singular solution (the envelope) is not in the C-family at all
