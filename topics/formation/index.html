formation algorithm

given an nnn-parameter family f(x,y,c1,…,cn)=0f(x, y, c_1, \dots, c_n) = 0f(x,y,c1​,…,cn​)=0:

  1. differentiate nnn times with respect to xxx (you get nnn new equations)
  2. eliminate the nnn parameters across the n+1n+1n+1 equations
  3. the resulting relation F(x,y,y′,…,y(n))=0F(x, y, y', \dots, y^{(n)}) = 0F(x,y,y′,…,y(n))=0 is the differential equation

common mistakes

typical mistakes

  1. forgetting that c1ex+c2c_1 e^{x+c_2}c1​ex+c2​ has one essential constant (A=c1ec2A = c_1 e^{c_2}A=c1​ec2​), not two. order = 1, not 2. ::

  2. differentiating fewer times than the number of parameters. each parameter needs one differentiation. ::

  3. eliminating constants incorrectly — use the original equation and all differentiated equations together as a system. ::

  4. thinking a singular solution is just a particular solution with C=0C=0C=0. it is not in the family at all. ::

practice

three hints, then the full solution — move only when you want to. every solution is checked by substituting back into the equation.

basic
Find the differential equation of the family of all circles which touch the x-axis at the origin, i.e. x2+(y−a)2=a2⟺x2+y2=2ay, where a is the single arbitrary parameter.

One parameter means differentiate **once**, then eliminate a using the original equation and its derivative together.
Solve for the parameter first: yx2+y2​=2a. Now differentiate that with respect to x — but do not throw away the y′ terms from the denominator.
y2y(2x+2yy′)−(x2+y2)y′​=0⇒2xy+y2y′−x2y′=0, and the y2y′ combines with −x2y′.

There is **one** parameter a, so differentiate once and eliminate it. **Step 1 — isolate the parameter.** x2+y2=2ay⟹yx2+y2​=2a. **Step 2 — differentiate.** y2y(2x+2yy′)−(x2+y2)y′​=0. **Step 3 — clear and simplify the numerator.** 2xy+2y2y′−x2y′−y2y′=0⟹2xy+y2y′−x2y′=0. **Step 4 — group.** (y2−x2)dxdy​+2xy=0​. The parameter a is gone, and the resulting equation is first order — as it must be, since the family had one parameter. *Verification:* with y=a±a2−x2​, so y′=∓a2−x2​x​ and y2−x2=2a(a±⋅​)∓…, direct symbolic substitution reduces (y2−x2)y′+2xy to 0 identically for every a and both branches.

basic
Find the differential equation of the family of all non-vertical straight lines y=mx+c, where m and c are arbitrary constants.

Count the parameters first: m and c are both arbitrary, so how many times must you differentiate?
y′=m kills m; y′′=0 kills c. The straightness of a line is expressed by its vanishing second derivative.
Differentiate twice: y′=m, then y′′=0. That relation has no constants left in it.

The family y=mx+c has **two** essential parameters, m and c, so the differential equation must be of order 2. Differentiate once: y′=m⟹m=y′. Differentiate a second time: y′′=0. Both m and c have disappeared without ever needing to be substituted back — the two differentiations have produced exactly the two equations needed to eliminate them. Hence the differential equation of the family of all straight lines is y′′=0, which has order 2 and degree 1. Geometrically this is exactly "curvature is zero everywhere". (Vertical lines are missed only because they cannot be written as y=f(x); that is why the question says *non-vertical*.)

basic
Form the differential equation of the one-parameter family y=ce2x. Give the equation in the form F(x,y,y′)=0.

One parameter c means order 1: differentiate exactly once.
dxdy​=2ce2x. Now compare this with the original equation — y=ce2x.
The two right-hand sides differ by a factor 2, so y′=2y, i.e. y′−2y=0.

One parameter c ⟹ differentiate once. y=ce2x⟹dxdy​=2ce2x. Now eliminate c without substituting it explicitly. Since y=ce2x, the derivative equation reads y′=2(ce2x)=2y. So the differential equation of the family is y′−2y=0, first order and first degree — as it must be for a one-parameter family. *Verification:* for y=ce2x, y′−2y=2ce2x−2ce2x=0 identically for every c.

JEE Main
Find the differential equation of the family of all parabolas whose axis is parallel to the x-axis: (y−k)2=4a(x−h), with h,k,a three arbitrary parameters.

Three parameters means differentiate three times. But do **not** try to expand (y−k)2 first — differentiate in the factored form where y−k appears as a single block.
First derivative: (y−k)y′=2a, so y−k=2a/y′. Second: (y′)2+(y−k)y′′=0. Notice a is already gone — you only need y−k.
Substitute y−k=−y′′(y′)2​ into the second derivative relation and clear the y′′: 3y′y′′−y′′(y′)2y′′′​=0.

Three parameters (h,k,a) ⟹ differentiate three times. Keep (y−k) unexpanded so it behaves as one block. **First derivative.** 2(y−k)y′=4a⟹(y−k)y′=2a⟹y−k=y′2a​. **Second derivative.** (y′)2+(y−k)y′′=0⟹y−k=−y′′(y′)2​. **Third derivative** (differentiate the second-derivative equation directly): 2y′y′′+y′y′′+(y−k)y′′′=0. **Substitute** y−k=−y′′(y′)2​: 3y′y′′−y′′(y′)2y′′′​=0. **Clear y′′:** 3(y′′)2−y′y′′′=0​, of order 3 and degree 1 — matching the three parameters h,k,a. *Verification:* writing y=k±2a(x−h)​ and differentiating through to y′′′, the residual 3(y′′)2−y′y′′′ simplifies to exactly 0 for several exact parameter triples, e.g. (a,k,h)=(1,21​,51​) and (2,−1,101​), on both branches.

The differential equation of the family of curves x2=4b(y+b),b∈R arbitrary, is:

There is one parameter b, so differentiate once. Key convention: b is a *constant of the family*, so b′=0 when you differentiate.
x2=4by+4b2⇒2x=4by′+0, hence b=2y′x​.
Put b=2y′x​ back into x2=4by+4b2, multiply by (y′)2 and divide by x: you land on x(y′)2=x+2yy′.

There is **one** parameter b, so differentiate once. **Step 1 — differentiate.** This is a family of parabolas with axis along the y-axis. Since b is an arbitrary *constant* of the family, it has derivative 0: 2x=4by′+8b⋅0=4by′⟹b=2y′x​. **Step 2 — substitute back** into x2=4by+4b2: x2=4(2y′x​)y+4(2y′x​)2=y′2xy​+(y′)2x2​. **Step 4 — multiply by (y′)2 and divide by x=0:** x2(y′)2=2xyy′+x2⟹x(y′)2=x+2yy′​. Hence option **(3)**. *Verification:* with y=4bx2​−b we get y′=2bx​, so x(y′)2=4b2x3​ and x+2yy′=x+2(4bx2​−b)2bx​=x+4b2x3​−x=4b2x3​. Identical, for every b.

JEE Main
Form the differential equation of the family of confocal, coaxial parabolas y2=4a(x+a), where a is the single arbitrary parameter. (This family is self-orthogonal — check that in your solution.)

One parameter a ⟹ differentiate once, and the derivative equation gives a in terms of x,y,y′ immediately. Do that first.
2yy′=4a⇒a=2yy′​. Put this back into y2=4a(x+a) — note the x+a also contains a, so a appears twice.
y2=4⋅2yy′​(x+2yy′​)=2xyy′+y2(y′)2. Divide by y.

One parameter a ⟹ differentiate once and eliminate a. **Step 1 — differentiate.** 2yy′=4a⟹a=2yy′​. **Step 2 — substitute back** into y2=4a(x+a), replacing *both* occurrences of a: y2=4(2yy′​)[x+2yy′​]=2yy′x+(yy′)2. **Step 3 — divide by y=0:** y(y′)2+2xy′−y=0​, order 1, degree 2. **Self-orthogonality.** Replace y′ by −y′1​=−dydx​: y(y′1​)2−y′2x​−y=0. Multiplying by (y′)2: y−2xy′−y(y′)2=0, which is exactly the *negative* of the original equation. So the family of orthogonal trajectories is the family itself — a **self-orthogonal** family. *Verification:* with y=±4a(x+a)​, symbolic substitution of y′ into y(y′)2+2xy′−y simplifies to exactly 0 for a=1,2,52​ on both branches.

JEE Advanced
The differential equation of the family of curves c(y+c)2=x3, where c is an arbitrary non-zero constant, can be written in the form 12y(y′)2+ax=bx(y′)3. Find a+b.

One parameter c ⟹ differentiate once: 2c(y+c)y′=3x2. Then **divide the derivative equation by the original** — that kills c and (y+c) at the same time.
c(y+c)22c(y+c)y′​=x33x2​⇒y+c2y′​=x3​⇒y+c=32xy′​.
Now c=y+c−y=32xy′​−y. Substitute **both** of these into the original equation and clear the denominators carefully.

**Step 1 — differentiate once.** 2c(y+c)y′=3x2. **Step 2 — divide the derivative equation by the original equation.** This is the move that removes c almost for free: c(y+c)22c(y+c)y′​=x33x2​⟹y+c2y′​=x3​⟹y+c=32xy′​. **Step 3 — recover c.** c=(y+c)−y=32xy′​−y=32xy′−3y​. **Step 4 — put both back** into c(y+c)2=x3: (32xy′−3y​)(32xy′​)2=x3. **Step 5 — clear denominators.** Multiply by 27 and by x2: (2xy′−3y)4x2(y′)2=27x3⟹(y′)2(2xy′−3y)=27x. **Step 6 — expand and rearrange.** 2x(y′)3−3y(y′)2=27x⟹12y(y′)2+27x=8x(y′)3. Comparing with 12y(y′)2+ax=bx(y′)3 gives a=27 and b=8, so a+b=27+8=35​. **Watch the constant factors.** The easy mistake is to write y+c=3xy′​ (dropping the 2) or to end at 12y(y′)2+9x=8x(y′)3 (forgetting the 4 from (2xy′)2). Both give a plausible-looking but wrong coefficient pair. *Verification:* with y=c​x3/2​−c one gets y′=2c​3x​​, and the residual 12y(y′)2+27x−8x(y′)3 simplifies to exactly 0 for c=1,2,31​ on both branches.

JEE Advanced
Find the differential equation of the family of all circles which pass through the origin and whose centre lies on the line y=x.

Write the general circle through the origin as x2+y2+2gx+2fy=0. Its centre is (−g,−f); impose the condition that this lies on y=x.
(−g)=(−f)⇒g=f. So the family is x2+y2+2g(x+y)=0 — one parameter, differentiate once.
2x+2yy′+2g(1+y′)=0⇒g=−1+y′x+yy′​. Substitute into the original and multiply by (1+y′).

**Step 1 — write the family.** A circle through the origin has no constant term: x2+y2+2gx+2fy=0, with centre (−g,−f). **Step 2 — impose "centre on y=x".** The centre must satisfy −g=−f, i.e. g=f. Writing the single parameter as g: x2+y2+2g(x+y)=0. One parameter ⟹ differentiate once. **Step 3 — differentiate.** 2x+2yy′+2g(1+y′)=0⟹g=−1+y′x+yy′​. **Step 4 — substitute** into the family equation: x2+y2−1+y′2(x+y)(x+yy′)​=0. **Step 5 — clear the denominator.** (x2+y2)(1+y′)−2(x+y)(x+yy′)=0. Expanding: x2+y2+(x2+y2)y′−2x2−2xy−2xyy′−2y2y′=0, (−x2+y2−2xy)+(x2−y2−2xy)y′=0. **Step 6 — rearrange into slope form.** (x2−y2−2xy)dxdy​=x2−y2+2xy​ Order 1, degree 1 — correct for a one-parameter family. *Verification:* the circles are x2+y2+2g(x+y)=0, so y=−g±g2−2gx−x2​. Substituting y and y′ into (x2−y2−2xy)y′−(x2−y2+2xy) simplifies to exactly 0 for g=1,2,−23​ on both branches. *Sanity check on the sign:* compare the easy special case "centre on the x-axis", x2+y2=2cx, which is the same computation with f=0; it reproduces dxdy​=2xyy2−x2​ from the tangent-bisector problem.
