before any formula — what does “separable” mean?

a differential equation is separable when you can write it as

f(x) dx=g(y) dyf(x)\,dx = g(y)\,dyf(x)dx=g(y)dy

all xxx‘s on one side, all yyy‘s on the other. then just integrate both sides.

predict before integrating dxdy​=(1+x)(1+y), what do you expect the shape of the solution family? straight lines? exponentials? something else?

standard form and solution

dydx=f(x)g(y)  ⟹  dyg(y)=f(x) dx  ⟹  ∫dyg(y)=∫f(x) dx+C\frac{dy}{dx} = f(x)g(y) \implies \frac{dy}{g(y)} = f(x)\,dx \implies \int \frac{dy}{g(y)} = \int f(x)\,dx + Cdxdy​=f(x)g(y)⟹g(y)dy​=f(x)dx⟹∫g(y)dy​=∫f(x)dx+C

crucial: the constant CCC is added at the moment of integration, not later. carrying it through algebraic steps is what keeps the family intact.

reducible to separable: ax+by+cax + by + cax+by+c

dydx=f(ax+by+c)\frac{dy}{dx} = f(ax + by + c)dxdy​=f(ax+by+c)

substitute t=ax+by+ct = ax + by + ct=ax+by+c. then dtdx=a+bdydx  ⟹  dydx=1b(dtdx−a)\frac{dt}{dx} = a + b\frac{dy}{dx} \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{b}\left(\frac{dt}{dx} - a\right)dxdt​=a+bdxdy​⟹dxdy​=b1​(dxdt​−a).

the equation becomes:

1b(dtdx−a)=f(t)  ⟹  dtdx=a+bf(t)  ⟹  dta+bf(t)=dx\frac{1}{b}\left(\frac{dt}{dx} - a\right) = f(t) \implies \frac{dt}{dx} = a + b f(t) \implies \frac{dt}{a + b f(t)} = dxb1​(dxdt​−a)=f(t)⟹dxdt​=a+bf(t)⟹a+bf(t)dt​=dx

now integrate both sides.

practice

three hints, then the full solution — move only when you want to. every solution is checked by substituting back into the equation.

basic
Solve dxdy​=(1+x)(1+y).

Factor the right side as f(x)g(y). If you can move every y-part to one side and every x-part to the other, the variables have separated.
You get 1+ydy​=(1+x)dx. Integrate both sides and add C at the moment you integrate — not afterwards.
ln∣1+y∣=x+2x2​+C, so 1+y=Aex+x2/2. Replace ±eC by a single arbitrary constant A and solve for y.

The right side factors: f(x)=1+x, g(y)=1+y. Divide both sides by 1+y: 1+ydy​=(1+x)dx Integrate, adding C at this moment: ln∣1+y∣=∫(1+x)dx=x+2x2​+C Exponentiate and fold the sign into the constant (A=±eC, any non-zero constant): 1+y=Aex+x2/2⟹y=Aex+x2/2−1​ **Check.** dxd​(Aex+x2/2−1)=Aex+x2/2(1+x)=(1+x)(1+y), which is the original right side. Good. **Caveat.** Dividing by 1+y loses the constant solution y=−1; substituting it back confirms it also satisfies the original equation.

Solve dxdy​=x+2y+11​.

The right side mixes x and y inside one linear expression, so you cannot separate yet. Look at the combination x+2y — that is the whole story.
Put u=x+2y+1 and use the chain rule: dxdu​=1+2dxdy​=1+u2​. That equation separates in u and x.
u+2u​du=dx integrates to u−2ln∣u+2∣=x+C. Substitute u=x+2y+1 back, and the lone x on the right cancels the one on the left.

This is the "f(ax+by+c)" pattern. Put u=x+2y+1 Chain rule (do not forget the product rule!): dxdu​=1+2dxdy​=1+u2​=uu+2​ Separate: u+2u​du=dx Integrate. Split u=(u+2)−2: ∫(1−u+22​)du=u−2ln∣u+2∣=x+C Back-substitute u=x+2y+1 (so u+2=x+2y+3): x+2y+1−2ln∣x+2y+3∣=x+C which simplifies to 2y+1−2ln∣x+2y+3∣=C​ **Check.** Differentiating the implicit relation gives 2y′−x+2y+32(1+2y′)​=0⟹y′(x+2y+3)=1+2y′⟹y′(x+2y+1)=1, so y′=x+2y+11​. The general solution of (1+x2)(1+y)dy+(1+y2)(1+x)dx=0 is:

Both dx and dy carry a product (1+x2)(1+y2)-type factor. Can you divide the whole equation by one product so that each term contains only one variable?
Divide by (1+x2)(1+y2): you get 1+y21+y​dy+1+x21+x​dx=0. Now each numerator must be split into two pieces over its denominator.
1+u21​+21​1+u22u​ gives arctanu+21​ln(1+u2). Apply that to both x and y.

Divide the whole equation by (1+x2)(1+y2) so that each term holds a single variable: 1+y21+y​dy+1+x21+x​dx=0 Split each fraction over its denominator: [1+y21​+21​1+y22y​]dy+[1+x21​+21​1+x22x​]dx=0 Integrate term by term (each bracket is one arctan plus one 21​ln): arctany+21​ln(1+y2)+arctanx+21​ln(1+x2)=C **Check.** Differentiating: 1+x21+x​+1+y2(1+y)y′​=0⟹y′=−(1+x2)(1+y)(1+y2)(1+x)​ which is precisely the original equation dxdy​=−(1+x2)(1+y)(1+y2)(1+x)​. Good. (One may also combine the two arctangents using arctanx+arctany=arctan1−xyx+y​, but the four-term form above is the safer answer.)
