before any formula — what does a differential equation mean?

a differential equation dydx=f(x,y)\frac{dy}{dx} = f(x, y)dxdy​=f(x,y) assigns a slope to every point (x,y)(x, y)(x,y) in the plane. draw a tiny line segment with that slope at many points — you get a slope field (or direction field).

predict look at the slope field for y′=x−y. where do you think the solutions flatten out (horizontal tangents)? where do they become vertical?

what the slope field tells you

  • isoclines: curves where the slope is constant (f(x,y)=cf(x,y) = cf(x,y)=c). along an isocline, all line segments have the same angle.
  • integral curves: solutions — curves tangent to the field at every point. they never cross the field.
  • existence & uniqueness: if f(x,y)f(x,y)f(x,y) is nice (Lipschitz in yyy), through every point there is exactly one solution curve.

common mistakes

typical mistakes

  1. confusing subtangent (∣y/y′∣|y/y'|∣y/y′∣) with subnormal (∣yy′∣|yy'|∣yy′∣). subtangent = projection on xxx-axis of tangent segment; subnormal = projection of normal. ::

  2. forgetting absolute values in length formulas. lengths are positive; the formulas use ∣y∣|y|∣y∣ and ∣y′∣|y'|∣y′∣. ::

  3. thinking the normal line has slope 1/y′1/y'1/y′. perpendicular slopes multiply to −1-1−1, so normal slope is −1/y′-1/y'−1/y′. ::

  4. assuming isoclines are solution curves. isoclines are curves of constant slope; solutions are tangent to the field. ::
