before any formula — what does “orthogonal” mean?

two curves are orthogonal at their intersection if their tangents are perpendicular (m1m2=−1m_1 m_2 = -1m1​m2​=−1).

an orthogonal trajectory of a given family of curves is a curve that intersects every member of the family at right angles.

common mistakes

typical mistakes

  1. replacing y′y'y′ with 1/y′1/y'1/y′ instead of −1/y′-1/y'−1/y′. perpendicular slopes multiply to −1-1−1, so y′→−1/y′y' \to -1/y'y′→−1/y′. ::

  2. forgetting to eliminate the parameter ccc before doing the orthogonal substitution. you must have a parameter-free DE first. ::

  3. in polar, using y′→−1/y′y' \to -1/y'y′→−1/y′ instead of dr/dθ→−r2dθ/drdr/d\theta \to -r^2 d\theta/drdr/dθ→−r2dθ/dr. ::

  4. thinking the orthogonal family has the same parameter. it has its own parameter (the constant of integration from solving the orthogonal DE). ::
